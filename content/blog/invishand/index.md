## The Invisible Hand has stuck its finger up my butt-hole and I do not care for it

This essay concerns what I have inferred from personal experience to be a somewhat less discussed aspect of the human condition; at least in the way I have come to think of it.

My central hypothesis is that in the course of progressing from a hunter-gatherer society to one of abundance, we have gradually replaced the chains of scarcity, and as of recent, the chains of tyrants, with chains that we have wrought ourselves. This is how I characterize these chains:
1.	We engage in manufacturing necessities of no practical implication. Thingamabobs that neither produce, nor automate and nor make our lives easier, but rather serve as symbols of status.
2.	In pursuit of these status symbols, we sacrifice integral aspects of ourselves. In essence, we sell ourselves out.

Of course this has always been recognized. What has gotten lesser recognition is the subsequent arms race from this. You can always choose to be the enlightened one and excuse yourself from this competition, but where does that live you? I can assure you that while you may not see the practical difference in dining at Intercontinental and then staying over there afterwards as opposed to your apartment in the outskirts of Dhaka, any partner of yours surely will, and she will choose accordingly.

Thus, you, dear reader, are left with a dilemma. Be bound by these chains while you are slowly ripped off your very being living nothing but a masked shell optimized for maximal social approval or free yourself off these chains and become an involuntary celibate. The choice, dear reader, is yours.
